Notes

- Deviations from the plans were recorded in the stage reports, not hidden. Two that matter for reading the table: in stage 6A the criteria for light-curve shape and colour were written after the shape and colour had already been seen in stage 5b (marked in `FLARES_PLAN.md`); in stage 6B the model-1 threshold was fixed after the download but before D was computed.
- Before each freeze, the only quantities computed for the targets were frame counts, visit counts, rejection counts per cut, saturation fractions, visit gaps, error sizes and the median and scatter of the difference between the two photometries.
- Stage 7: the registered rule for choosing the spectral model stepped from one model to the next and stopped at the absorbed power law, although the reflection model fits better by 36.7 in AIC; this was reported as a deviation and both are shown side by side. Stage 8: one planned variant of the null test was not run because no bias was found, and a linear time term was added to the direct comparison after the plan.
- Stage 9: (1) the overall label was first computed by comparing the pairs of cell labels of the two samples, which gave "differs between the samples"; the wording of the plan (a label per sample first, then the comparison) gives "depends on the model", and the script was corrected. The four cell labels are the same in both runs. (2) The plan asks for the number of objects with a residual beyond 3σ expected under the null; the statistic was missing from the first null run, was added, and the null samples were remade with the same seeds. (3) One identifiability criterion passed exactly at its boundary (an error of −10 d against "within 10 d", on a 5 d grid). (4) That the synthetic samples are rougher than the data was learned from the results; the registered verdicts were applied as written. (5) The comparison with Mandal et al. (2024) is post hoc and has no null distribution. (6) Page numbers of Lyu et al. (2019) are those of the arXiv v2 PDF.
- Stage 10: (1) the ZTF files of 75 type 1 objects had been downloaded in stage 5b and were reused; 225 files of 114 objects were new. (2) One request (the g band of one object) failed four times (time-outs of 600 s or 504 responses) and was counted as "no data"; that object uses the r band. (3) During the download a second process was attached for seven minutes and one request was tried in another form; all files kept are of the registered request form. (4) The seeing flags were completed after the freeze, when the exposure metadata had arrived (52 → 56 flagged objects); the objects used, the driver bands and the hashes did not change. (5) In the synthetic samples the fitted jitter variance was also subtracted from the optical amplitude; decided before the freeze. (6) A one-line error in the summary script was fixed before any number came out. (7) Added after the real fit and kept out of the labels (post hoc): the sample median of χ² per degree of freedom, the 95% intervals from resampling the objects (only that of q was registered), the equations of Mandal et al. (2024) before the disc correction, their rest-frame conversion and a shift of log L by ±0.3 dex. (8) The labels were made with the equations registered (after the disc correction); the comparison that matches a fit without an undelayed term is the one before the correction (0.63 and 0.57 instead of 0.57 and 0.50; the label does not change). (9) The comparison "converted to the same luminosity definition" was not made, because the definition of the BASS bolometric luminosity was not confirmed at the time. (It was read in the paper afterwards, on 2026-11-19: 8 times the intrinsic 14–150 keV luminosity, Koss et al. 2022, Section 4.4. The conversion to a 5100 Å-based luminosity was still not made.) (10) In the null samples with the fitted lags, negative lags were entered as zero. (11) The coverage of the joint-fit interval (0.81) fell outside the registered range (0.58–0.78) and is recorded as "interval not calibrated". (12) The example objects of the figure were chosen by a rule written in the plotting script, not in the plan. (13) That the synthetic null samples are cleaner than the data was learned from the results; the registered labels were applied as written.
- This repository has more than one commit from 2026-11-18 on; the first commit holds the state up to the stage 7 plan, the second adds stages 7, 7b and 8 in one step, the third adds stage 9 in one step, the fourth adds stage 10 in one step. The order within them is given by the table above.
- Commit timestamps are set by the committing machine. The push times recorded by the remote are the independent record of the order; they are part of the original history that can be shown on request.
